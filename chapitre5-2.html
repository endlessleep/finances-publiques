5.2 — Public Finance Management (PFM)

Chapter 5: Central and Local Government Public Finance

The Three Objectives (Layers) of PFM

Public Finance Management (PFM) is the technical framework used to ensure public funds are handled effectively. It can be understood through three distinct, yet overlapping objectives:

1. Fiscal Discipline

Goal: Economy. Ensuring that total spending stays within the budget limits and avoids excessive deficits.

2. Purposeful Allocation

Goal: Effectiveness. Directing resources toward the priorities that matter most to society (e.g., shifting funds from administration to education).

3. Value for Money

Goal: Efficiency. Getting the maximum possible "output" for every unit of "input" spent.

Layer 1: Fiscal Discipline (Stability)

Fiscal discipline is the foundation of economic stability. Without it, a country risks inflation, currency crises, or debt defaults.

The Tools of Discipline:

  • Fiscal Rules: Legal limits on deficits or debt (e.g., the EU's 3% deficit limit).
  • Fiscal Council: Independent bodies that monitor government promises and predict if they are realistic.
  • Top-Down Budgeting: The central government sets a "hard ceiling" for total spending before ministries start negotiating their individual shares.
🏮 Fiscal Risks: Modern PFM requires constant "Fiscal Risk Analysis" — assessing how natural disasters, wars, or banking collapses might blow a hole in the budget.

Layer 2: Purposeful Allocation (Strategic)

It is not enough to stay within the budget; the money must be spent on the right things. This is the strategic layer of public finance.

Programme Budgeting: Instead of giving a ministry money for "salaries" or "rent", the government funds specific programs (e.g., "Reduction of Child Poverty"). Each program must have clear objectives and be connected to a long-term strategy.

To achieve purposeful allocation, governments use a Medium-Term Budgetary Framework (MTBF). This ensures that a decision made today (like building a hospital) takes into account the costs of running it for the next 4-5 years, not just the construction cost this year.

Layer 3: Value for Money (Efficiency)

This final layer asks: "Are we using our resources efficiently?" It is the most technically complex objective to achieve.

Performance Budgeting

Budgets are linked to Key Performance Indicators (KPIs). If a school receives more money, we expect to see an improvement in student grades or graduation rates.

Spending Reviews

A systematic analysis of existing spending to identify "waste" or "inefficiency". These are not just "budget cuts"; they are targeted removals of programs that no longer work.

International Benchmarks:

  • Canada: Uses "Departmental Plans" to transparently show citizens what each dollar achieves.
  • Austria: Uses Wirkungsziele (Impact Goals) integrated directly into the budget law.
  • The Netherlands: Famous for rigorous "Spending Reviews" focused on long-term efficiency.

PFM Summary:

  • Discipline: Stay under the ceiling (Rules & Top-Down).
  • Allocation: Spend on priorities (Programs & MTBF).
  • Efficiency: Get results for your money (Performance & Reviews).
  • Modern budgeting is multi-year and outcome-driven.

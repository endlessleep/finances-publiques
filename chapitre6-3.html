6.3 — PAYG vs. Funded Systems

Chapter 6: The Economics of Old-Age Pensions

The PAYG Pipe: How It Works

In a Pay-As-You-Go (PAYG) system, young workers directly pay for the older retirees right now. There is absolutely no savings account — every dollar the government takes from a 25-year-old today is immediately handed to a 75-year-old tomorrow.

The Mathematical Balancing Act:

$\tau \times w \times N = b \times R$

Where: $\tau$ = tax rate, $w$ = average salary, $N$ = total number of young workers, $b$ = monthly check for each retiree, $R$ = total number of retired people.

Rewriting it to see the problem:

$\frac{b}{w} = \tau \times \frac{N}{R}$

This proves that the size of the grandfather's check ($b/w$) depends entirely on how many young workers exist compared to old people ($N/R$). If the number of older people explodes, the government is mathematically forced to either cut the pension checks or savagely raise taxes on the young.

The Hidden Profit of the Pipe (PAYG Return)

What profit do you actually "earn" when you pay into this pipe your whole life? Unlike a bank, there is no interest rate. Your profit depends entirely on the country's biology and economy:

The Magic Rule of Return:

$(1 + r_{PAYG}) \approx 1 + n + g$

🎯 What It Means: $r_{PAYG} \approx n + g$ — The profit you make from a PAYG system equals the population growth ($n$) plus the country's salary growth ($g$). If your country has lots of babies and everyone is getting richer, your pension will be huge. If no one has babies and salaries are frozen, your pension will be terrible.

The Pros and Cons of the PAYG Pipe

✅ Instant Start

You can turn it on immediately. You don't have to wait 40 years to save money in a bank; you just tax the young today and feed the old today.

✅ Total Safety

It provides perfect protection against bad luck. If you live to be 100, or if inflation hits, the government simply takes more taxes from the young to keep you alive.

✅ Kindness (Redistribution)

The rules can be twisted to help the poor. A rich banker and a poor cleaner pay taxes, but the government can easily give the cleaner a larger share to keep her out of poverty.

✅ Immune to Stock Crashes

Your money is totally safe from Wall Street. When the 2008 Financial Crisis destroyed millions of private bank accounts, PAYG grandpas didn't lose a single penny.

❌ The Biology Trap

Remember the magic rule ($n + g$)? That rule starts crashing when the country stops having babies — which is exactly what is happening in Europe right now.

❌ Political Lies

The government can secretly change the rules at any time. They can promise you a great retirement today, but violently cut your checks in 20 years if they run out of money.

❌ Zero Real Savings

Because the young money is instantly eaten by the old, the country never builds a giant productive savings account, which can slow down the entire economy.

❌ The Pyramid Scheme

PAYG works exactly like an illegal Ponzi scheme. It only survives if you constantly add more new young people at the bottom. Without new babies, it collapses.

Funded (Capitalised) Systems: True Savings

In a funded system, your tax money is not given to an old person. It belongs to you. It is locked in a real bank and invested in the stock market until you retire.

The Profit Rule:

$r_{funded} \approx r$

Your profit ($r$) depends entirely on how well your stocks, bonds, and real estate perform.

These genuine savings systems usually take three forms:

The Solo Account (DC)

You have your own personal account with your name on it. Whatever amount of money is in there on your 65th birthday is all you get. The worker takes 100% of the risk. If the market crashes, you starve. Examples: American 401(k), Australian Superannuation.

The Boss's Promise (Funded DB)

Your boss promises you a perfect monthly salary for life. The boss is forced to invest millions in the stock market to make sure he can afford it. The boss takes 100% of the risk. Used heavily by old British and American factories.

The Beautiful Fake (NDC)

It is a clever trick used by Sweden and Italy. You get a piece of paper that looks exactly like a personal account, but there is no real money inside. It is actually just the old PAYG pipe disguised as a private account to trick politicians into respecting the math.

The Ultimate Choice: Defined Benefit (DB) vs. Defined Contribution (DC)

Understanding the difference between these two systems is the most important lesson in modern pensions.

The Rule The Promise (DB) The Solo Account (DC)
What do you get? A strict math formula based on your final salary (e.g., "$2,000 every month"). You just get whatever cash happens to be in your account when you retire.
Who suffers if the market crashes? The Boss or the Government. They must magically find the cash to pay you anyway. You. You lose everything and your pension is destroyed.
Changing Jobs Terrible. If you quit your job after 5 years, you usually lose all your nice pension promises. Perfect. It's your bank account. You just take your money to the next job.
Is it fair to the poor? Yes. The rules are often rewritten to give extra to poor workers. No. If you were poor your whole life, your account is tiny, and you retire poor.
📊 The Global Shift: Because the "Boss's Promise" (DB) was too expensive and risky, almost all modern countries are forcing young workers into Solo Accounts (DC). This means governments are completely pushing the terrifying risk of surviving old age onto the shoulders of normal citizens.

The Pros and Cons of True Savings (Funded)

✅ Safe from Demographics

Because your money comes from Apple or Tesla stock, it doesn't matter if nobody has babies anymore. Your cash is safe from the fertility crash.

✅ Massive Economic Power

All this saved money builds a terrifyingly huge mountain of wealth for the country, which banks use to build new factories and technology.

✅ Total Transparency

You can check your personal balance every morning on your phone. No politician can lie to you about how much money is left.

✅ Rich Returns

Historically, the stock market creates a massive 4% to 7% profit every year, which makes you vastly richer than the slow PAYG pipe.

❌ The Double-Payment Nightmare

If you switch from PAYG to a Funded system today, the young workers are forced to pay twice: once to save for their own account, and once to keep the current old people alive. It is politically impossible.

❌ The Deadly Casino

You bear all the market risk. If a new war starts the day before you retire, your life savings are gone. Also, if you live too long, your account hits zero.

❌ Brutal Inequality

True savings systems do not care about kindness, only math. If you are a poor worker, you will be a poor grandpa. There is no safety net.

❌ Banker Corruption

Giant piles of money attract sharks. Bad management, lazy bankers, or secret fees can easily steal millions from the citizens without them noticing.

Chapter 6.3 — PAYG vs Funded:

  • PAYG (The Pipe): Your profit is strictly $n + g$ (population growth + wage growth). It is instantly ready and protects you from Wall Street, but it dies when people stop having babies.
  • Funded (Real Savings): Your profit is simply $r$ (stock market return). It grows massive wealth and ignores the baby crisis, but it leaves you totally exposed to terrifying market crashes and banker fees.
  • The Promise (DB) vs Your Account (DC): DB forces the boss/state to take all the risk. DC forces YOU to take all the risk. The entire world is rapidly switching to DC to save the government money.

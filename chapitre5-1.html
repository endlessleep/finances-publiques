5.1 — Role & Pressures of Public Budgets

Chapter 5: Central and Local Government Public Finance

The Size of the Modern State

To understand the modern state, we look at the Tax-to-GDP ratio. This number shows the percentage of a nation's total money that the government collects through taxes to pay for public services.

Real World History (Tax/GDP Trends):

Before 1900, the government was very small. But during the 20th century, especially after 1945, there was a massive explosion in government spending. Today, we see big differences depending on where you live:

  • Europe: Taxes are very high (often above 40%). Europeans believe in a strong Welfare State that takes care of its citizens.
  • United States: Taxes are much lower than in Europe. Americans prefer relying on private businesses rather than the government.
  • Developing Countries: Usually have very low taxes, though countries like China are catching up fast by spending huge amounts on roads and science (R&D).
⚖️ The Big Trade-Off: A large government can fix big problems (like poverty or pollution), but it strongly risks becoming lazy and wasting money if it isn't managed with strict discipline.

What Do We Expect from the Government?

Today, the "Social State" (or "Welfare State") doesn't just build roads; its main job is taking money from the rich to help the poor (redistribution) and protecting people from bad luck (insurance against risk).

Sector Main Job The Economic Reason
Social Protection Pays for old-age pensions, unemployment money, and disability help — this is the heart of the Welfare State. Redistribution (sharing wealth) and Risk Insurance (saving people from sudden ruin).
Health Makes sure everyone can see a doctor, even if they are poor or already sick. Positive Consumption Externality: a healthy population is a huge blessing for the whole country (less disease, stronger workers).
Education From elementary school to university — the state pays for citizens to get smarter. Investment in Human Capital: educating people creates massive wealth and positive benefits for the future economy.
General Operations The army, the police, judges, and government offices — the classic "Regalian" jobs. Pure Public Goods: things everyone shares (non-rival, non-excludable). Only the government can be trusted to build these.

Famous economist Piketty (2020) explains that the modern state completely changed its identity: it evolved from being simply a "Police & Army State" into a "Caring Social State" (health, schools, and welfare).

The Triple Bind (Barber, 2015)

Modern politicians face a brutal trap called the Triple Bind. This means they are fighting three powerful, opposing forces that make ruling a country incredibly difficult today.

1. Revenue Pressure (Taxes)

Because the world is so connected, governments are terrified to raise taxes. If they do, rich people and big companies will simply move their money to other countries.

2. Debt Crisis

After many historical crashes, governments are under extreme pressure to pay off their gigantic debts so they don't go bankrupt and lose the trust of world banks.

3. Demand for Perfection

Citizens constantly demand better and faster services (like perfect hospitals and ultra-fast internet) and complain instantly if the government looks lazy.

The Barber Paradox: Politicians are trapped. They must deliver better services while surviving on less borrowed money, all while being completely unable to simply raise taxes. The only way to survive this trap is by becoming brilliantly efficient and focusing purely on Value for Money.

The Future Threats to the Budget (2025-2050)

The government is not getting smaller; huge new global emergencies are forcing it to spend billions more just to survive.

The Robinson Theory (2020): Marc Robinson warns that government spending will explode massively over the next 30 years, driven by these unstoppable forces:

  • The Gray Tsunami (Aging Population): People are living much longer and having fewer babies. This means a tiny group of young workers runs the economy while millions of grandpas need expensive pensions and hospitals.
  • Climate Disaster: Trying to save the Earth requires gigantic investments in giant solar farms and protecting cities from floods. Private businessmen cannot afford this alone.
  • Crumbling Concrete: The bridges, train tracks, and water pipes built in the 1900s are rotting and breaking. Fixing this old infrastructure will be incredibly expensive.
🎖️ The 2025 Update: Since recent wars broke out across the globe, we now must urgently add Defense and Strategic Protection (like stockpiling gas and microchips) to the list of things the government must buy.

Rules for Modern Budgeting

To survive the Triple Bind, politicians must completely change how they spend our money. The new modern system relies on five strict rules:

  1. Focus on Results

    Old budgeting asks: "Did we spend $1 million on schools?" Modern budgeting demands: "Did the students actually get smarter?" We must stop bragging about hiring people, and start focusing on actual victories. Spending more money doesn't guarantee success.

  2. Watch the Whole Country

    Public finance must monitor every single mayor and town, not just the President. If the capital city acts responsible while small villages secretly borrow billions, the entire country is still in danger.

  3. Look 5 Years Ahead

    One-year budgets are a disaster — they make politicians greedy and blind. A Medium-Term Budgetary Framework (MTBF) forces the government to plan its money 3–5 years in the future, guaranteeing that smart investments (like building a university) aren't randomly cancelled next year.

  4. Trust the Managers

    Instead of treating school principals like children and telling them exactly how to spend every single penny, modern budgeting gives them freedom. In exchange, they face harsh punishments if they fail to improve student grades.

  5. Never Stop Judging

    Every single government program must be constantly tested and judged. If a program is wasting money, it must be aggressively destroyed. If we never test anything, stupid old ideas will accidentally survive forever.

💡 The Conclusion: These five smart rules are the only weapons we have to defeat the Triple Bind. You cannot give the citizens more quality using less money unless you ruthlessly test results and plan for the deep future.

Chapter 5.1 — The Big Picture:

  • States have evolved from "Police & Army" to a "Caring Social State" (health, schools, welfare) — this is the key lesson from Piketty (2020).
  • Barber's Triple Bind captures the brutal trap: governments face endless demands for better services, frightening debts, and laws blocking them from raising taxes — all simultaneously.
  • Robinson (2020) proves our bills are exploding soon because of: aging grandpas, climate disasters, and rotting bridges — to which we now add military defense after recent global wars.
  • The only way to survive is to switch from "lazy spending" to "brilliant performing" — the five rules of modern budgeting are our only hope.

5.1 — Role & Pressures of Public Budgets

Chapter 5: Central and Local Government Public Finance

The Scale of the Modern State

Understanding the modern state requires analyzing the Tax-to-GDP ratio. This metric reveals the percentage of a nation's total economic output that is collected through taxes to fund public services.

Empirical Observation (Tax/GDP Trends):

Historically, the role of the state was minimal before 1900. Throughout the 20th century, especially after 1945, there was an explosion in public spending. Today, we observe clear regional differences:

  • Europe: Characterized by high taxation levels (often above 40%), reflecting a strong commitment to the Welfare State.
  • United States: Maintains a lower level of taxation compared to Europe, favoring private market mechanisms.
  • Emerging Economies: Often show even lower levels, though countries like China are catching up rapidly in infrastructure and R&D investment.
⚖️ The Macro-Tradeoff: A larger state allows for better correction of externalities and social redistribution, but it also carries the risk of inefficiency if resources are not managed with strict discipline.

What Do We Expect from the State?

The "Social State" or "Welfare State" is not just about producing goods; it is primarily about redistribution and insurance against risk.

Sector Primary Role Economic Logic
Social Protection Covers pensions, unemployment benefits, and disability payments — the backbone of the Welfare State. Redistribution (from high to low earners) and Risk Insurance (protecting against life shocks).
Health Ensures universal access to medical care, regardless of income or pre-existing conditions. Positive Consumption Externality: a healthy population benefits everyone (lower contagion, higher productivity).
Education From primary school to university — the state funds access to knowledge and skills. Investment in Human Capital: education generates huge positive externalities for the entire economy.
General Services Public administration, national defense, police, justice system — the core "Regalian" functions. Pure Public Goods: non-rival, non-excludable — only the state can provide these.

According to Piketty (2020), the rise of the modern state is defined by the shift from a "Regalian State" (police, defense) to a "Social State" (health, education, transfers).

The Triple Bind (Barber, 2015)

Modern governments face a structural conflict known as the Triple Bind. This represents three contradictory pressures that make fiscal management extremely difficult in the 21st century.

1. Revenue Pressure

Global competition creates a downward pressure on the ability of states to raise taxes without losing mobile capital or talent.

2. Debt Constraints

Following historical crises, there is an urgent need to reduce debt burdens to maintain fiscal sustainability and credit ratings.

3. Demand for Quality

Citizens continuously demand improved quality and efficiency in public service delivery (health, digital infrastructure, etc.).

The Barber Paradox: Maintaining fiscal sustainability requires delivering more (higher quality services) with less (reduced debt) while operating under a structural ceiling on income (tax competition). This creates an absolute necessity for Value for Money reforms and performance-based management.

Anticipated Spending Pressures (2025-2050)

The state is not shrinking; if anything, new global challenges are forcing it to expand in strategic directions.

The Robinson Framework (2020): Marc Robinson anticipates that government spending across advanced nations will grow significantly over the next three decades, driven by several structural forces:

  • Demographic Shift (Aging Populations): As life expectancy rises and birth rates fall, the ratio of workers to retirees shrinks dramatically. This means escalating pension payments and healthcare costs that cannot be avoided without fundamental reform.
  • Climate Transformation: Addressing climate change demands massive public investment in renewable energy infrastructure, building retrofits, and carbon capture. The private sector alone cannot bear the transition costs.
  • Infrastructure Decay: In many advanced nations, transport networks, water systems, and digital infrastructure built in the 20th century are reaching the end of their lifespan and require costly renewal.
🎖️ The 2025 Additions: Since the recent geopolitical shifts, we must add Defense and Strategic Resilience (energy security, supply chain independence) to the list of non-negotiable spending priorities.

Principles of Modern Budgeting

To survive the triple bind, the way we "do" budgeting must change. Modern public finance management relies on five pillars:

  1. Performance Oriented

    Traditional budgeting asks "how much did we spend?" Modern budgeting asks "what did we achieve?" The focus shifts from inputs (how many teachers hired) to outcomes (how much did student scores improve). Without this shift, spending more money doesn't guarantee better results.

  2. Holistic (All Levels)

    Public finance must encompass all levels of government — central, regional, and local. If only the central government practices fiscal discipline while municipalities spend freely, the overall fiscal picture is misleading.

  3. Multi-Year Planning

    Annual budgets create short-termism: politicians spend now and leave the consequences for the next government. A Medium-Term Budgetary Framework (MTBF) plans spending over 3–5 years, ensuring that long-term investments (infrastructure, R&D) are not sacrificed for short-term political gains.

  4. Managerial Responsibility

    Instead of rigidly telling each ministry exactly how to spend every euro, modern budgeting gives managers more flexibility in exchange for more accountability. This means less micromanagement from the Ministry of Finance, but stricter evaluation of results.

  5. Constantly Evaluated

    Every program should be subject to regular evaluation. This includes spending reviews (are we getting value for money?), audits, and KPI tracking. Without evaluation, bad policies survive indefinitely simply because "we've always done it this way."

💡 Connecting to Barber: These five principles are exactly the tools needed to escape the Triple Bind. You cannot deliver more with less unless you measure performance, plan ahead, empower managers, and ruthlessly evaluate what works and what doesn't.

Chapter 5.1 — The Big Picture:

  • States have evolved from "Regalian" (police, defense) to "Social" (health, education, transfers) — this is the key insight from Piketty (2020).
  • Barber's Triple Bind captures the structural tension: governments face higher demand for services, higher debt burdens, and limited ability to raise taxes — all at the same time.
  • Robinson (2020) identifies the coming pressures: aging, climate, and infrastructure — to which we now add defense and strategic resilience after the 2022–2025 geopolitical shifts.
  • The only way to survive this is to move from "spending" to "performing" — the five principles of modern budgeting are the roadmap.

Chapter 6: The Economics of Old-Age Pensions

The Math of Aging

The absolute most important number in all of pension economics is the Old-Age Dependency Ratio (OADR). It tells us exactly how many retired grandpas each young worker is forced to carry on their back.

The Brutal Math Formula:

$\text{OADR} = \frac{\text{Sleeping People (Age 65+)}}{\text{Working People (Age 15–64)}} \times 100$

Simply put: How heavy is the burden on the youth?

Why This Terrifies Politicians

In a standard Pay-As-You-Go (PAYG) system, the young workers' taxes pay 100% of the older people's checks today. If the OADR goes up, it means fewer young people are paying for more older people. You either must slash the pension checks, or savagely raise taxes on the young.

The Shock of History

When Germany invented the modern pension in 1889, they cleverly set the retirement age to 70. Almost nobody lived to be 70! But today, a 65-year-old German easily expects to live for 20 more years. The system was never designed to pay a person to stay home for three decades.

The Disappearing Babies (1960–2023)

The root disease of the pension problem is Fertility. If a country stops making babies today, it will have a catastrophic lack of young workers in 30 years.

The Sad Reality (Eurostat Data):

  • In the 1960s, families in Europe easily had 2+ children each. Today, almost all of Europe is trapped at 1.3 to 1.5. A number of 2.1 is strictly required just to stop the country from slowly dying.
  • The map is deeply divided: France is surviving somewhat better (1.7), while the sunny beaches and snowy mountains of Italy, Spain, and the Czech Republic have crashed terribly to 1.2 giving them zero future workers.
  • A tiny spark of hope happened in the 2000s, but since 2010, the birth rates have plummeted again, proving this is a permanent cultural shift.
⚠️ The Ultimate Truth: No babies = a structurally broken pension system. This isn't a temporary bad luck streak — it is a permanent change in human history. The old PAYG trick is mathematically doomed.

Does Being Old Auto-Mean High Spending?

If a country is full of old people, does it automatically mean the tax bill is gigantic?

The Data Check: If we map it out, yes, there is a middle-strength connection. Countries with gray hair tend to have big tax bills. But the connection is not pure magic.

The Magic of Japan: Japan is 49% old, yet they only spend 9% of their wealth on pensions. Why didn't they burst into flames? Because of strict culture and smart laws. They forced older people to keep working longer, and rewrote the laws to automatically cut checks as the population shrank (NDC style).

💡 The Deep Insight: Biology hurts, but laws save you. If you aggressively fix your laws today (like Japan or Sweden), you can survive the aging tsunami perfectly. If you try to act generous with an unreformed system (Italy), you will slowly drown in debt.

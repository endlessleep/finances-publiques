7.3 — Healthcare Systems

Chapter 7: Health Economics — Theory, Systems, and the Cost of Aging

The Four Healthcare Systems

Every country builds its healthcare system differently. We can divide all global systems into four base models depending on who pays for it and who owns the hospitals. Most modern countries mix these together, but you must learn the four pure types.

Model Name Who Pays? Who Owns the Hospital? Examples
Beveridge Taxes The Government UK (NHS), Spain, Nordic countries
Bismarck Social Insurance (Workers/Bosses) Mix of Public and Private Germany, France, Japan, Czech Republic
National Health Insurance Taxes Private doctors Canada, Taiwan, South Korea
Out-of-Pocket You pay yourself Private doctors Many poor countries
USA (The Mess) A mix of everything A mix of everything Medicare, employer plans, millions with no insurance

🇬🇧 Beveridge Model (UK)

Created by William Beveridge in 1942. The government uses your regular tax money to build hospitals and pay doctors directly. When you go to the hospital, you pay absolutely nothing. The great strength is that everyone is equal, and it is very cheap to run. The weakness is that because it is free, people rush to use it, causing massive wait lists.

🇩🇪 Bismarck Model (Germany/Czech)

Created by Otto von Bismarck in 1883. Both you and your boss are forced to pay money into a health fund. You then use this fund to buy care from a mix of private and public doctors. The great strength is that patients are incredibly happy (they get to choose their own doctor quickly). The weakness is that the paperwork costs a lot of money, and it is difficult for people who don't have a job.

Which System is Better?

So, which is the perfect model? Economists looked at data from 2025 and found that there is no perfect winner.

✅ Why UK/Sweden is Good (Beveridge)

These systems are incredibly cheap to run. Because there rests only one giant "company" (the government), they do not waste money on advertising or crazy paperwork. In the UK, office paperwork only burns 2% of the total budget, while the USA burns almost 20%!

✅ Why Germany/Czech is Good (Bismarck)

Patients are much happier. In countries like Switzerland, 91% of people love their system. You can choose any doctor you want, and you don't have to wait 6 months for a simple knee survey. It feels fast and premium.

📊 The 2025 Truth: A massive study of 38 rich nations proved that turning public hospitals into private businesses does NOT magically fix everything. Statistically, people in the UK (Beveridge) live slightly longer, but the mathematical difference is so small it is basically a tie.

  • In the 1970s, the USA and Europe spent the exact same amount. Then the USA lost total control.
  • Even the most expensive European countries (like Switzerland) only spend half of what the US spends.
  • The Deep "US Paradox": Even though they spend the most money in world history, Americans die younger than Europeans, and their babies die more often. Money does not equal health perfectly.

The Money is Running Out

Health spending is growing terrifyingly fast. Almost every single country is watching hospital bills destroy their national budget.

📈 The Scary Fact: Healthcare is the only big government cost that strictly rises in every single country. Things like unemployment money or school spending sometimes go down, but healthcare always gets vastly more expensive every year.

If we look to the year 2070, what exactly will break the banks of these governments?

  • Pensions for the Old — The number one reason.
  • Healthcare — Constantly rising. Remember Grossman: as everyone gets really old ($\delta_t$ rises), keeping their bodies alive is insanely expensive.
  • Nursing Homes (Long-term care) — Because the 80+ crowd is exploding, paying for round-the-clock nurses will cost billions.
  • Schools — This actually might drop slightly because nobody is having babies anymore, which saves the government a little bit of money.

Who Pays The Bill?

There are three ways to pay for healthcare: Tax money, Forced Boss/Worker funds (social insurance), and Your own pocket. Let's look at how the world divides this:

🇸🇪 Nordic Countries (Tax)

Here, the government controls everything. Almost 90% of the cost is paid by simple taxes. Normal people pay almost nothing from their own pockets when they go to the hospital.

🇩🇪 Europe (Fund)

Here, they use the social fund system. Your boss covers part of the cost, and you cover the rest. The government just stands back and makes sure the funds play by the rules.

🇺🇸 USA (Chaos)

The private system is gigantic here. Older people get government money (Medicare), poor people get different government money (Medicaid), rich people get boss-money, and millions of people have absolutely nothing. It is a highly complex, very expensive mess.

🌍 Poorer Countries

Here, it is very sad: Out-of-pocket money rules everything. If you are poor and get sick in these countries, the hospital bill will completely destroy your family's savings.

Why Must The Government Get Involved?

Why can't we just buy health like we buy a normal pizza? Why must the government control it? Because health is not a normal product:

  • Market Crash: As we learned in Chapter 7.2, left alone, the health market instantly crashes due to adverse selection (the death spiral) and doctors tricking patients.
  • The Law: The only way to stop the "death spiral" is for the government to create a strict law forcing everyone (even healthy people) to buy insurance.
  • Because it is "Right": As a society, we simply agree that nobody should die because they are poor.
  • Robin Hood: Health insurance perfectly takes money from the rich and healthy, and strictly gives it to the poor and sick.
  • More Money: A healthy worker is a fast worker. If the government cures your sickness, you go back to work, and the country's economy (GDP) grows bigger.
⚠️ The Danger: Government hospitals have their own problems. They often suffer from massive moral hazard — patients treat the free hospital like a hotel, and politicians spend too much tax money just to win votes.

Efficiency: Does Spending More Give You Longer Life?

This is the greatest shock in health economics: spending more money does NOT make you live longer.

46% True

Link between money & life if we delete the USA

Most rich countries have a life expectancy of 75 to 85 years. But when researchers look at the data, the connection between "spending billions" and "living longer" is very weak. This is entirely because of America — they spend double the money of Europe, but die far sooner.

💡 The Golden Rule: It does not matter how much money you spend. It only matters how smart you are with it. Efficiency is the only thing that saves lives.

The Nightmare — Scarcity

Humans want to live forever, but the government only has a limited amount of money. Every dollar spent on hospitals is a dollar stolen from schools or roads. We call this the opportunity cost.

⚙️ Technical Efficiency (Doing it fast)

This means getting the maximum result for the cheapest price. Have we found a way to do surgery using 3 nurses instead of 5? Have we found a cheaper bandage? This is about removing pure waste.

📊 Allocative Efficiency (Doing the right thing)

This means putting money where it actually saves lives. Have we spent $10 million on a magic robot for rich people, while 1,000 poor people die because we didn't buy $5 vaccines? This is about making hard choices.

The Final Trade-Off: Fairness vs. Speed

  • Horizontal Fairness: If two people have the exact same broken arm, they both get the exact same bandage, no matter who is rich or poor.
  • Vertical Fairness: If someone is dying of cancer, they get far more money and time than someone who just has a small cough.

In real life, trying to be perfectly fair usually destroys efficiency, and being perfectly fast destroys fairness. Governments fight over this balance every single day.

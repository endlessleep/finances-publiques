4.2 — Externalities

Chapter 4: Public Goods & Externalities

What are Externalities?

An externality is a side-effect (a cost or a benefit) that happens when someone's action has an unpaid effect on a third person. In simple words, the person doing the action doesn't pay for the damage they cause (negative) or doesn't get paid for the help they give (positive).

Clear examples:

  • Negative: A steel factory that dumps chemical waste into a river where people swim. The factory saves money on garbage disposal, but the innocent town pays for the dirty water.
  • Positive: Beehives owned by a honey maker. The bees naturally fly over to the neighbor's apple farm and help pollinate the trees for free, making the neighbor richer.
⚠️ Because these side-effects are NOT included in the shop price, they trick the free market into making bad, inefficient choices (market failure). The shop price simply doesn't show the "true" cost to society.

Grouping Externalities

Externalities are usually sorted by their impact and their source:

Positive vs. Negative

Does the action accidentally create a blessing or a harm for others?

Production vs. Consumption

Does the side-effect happen while building the product in a factory or while a customer is using it?

Advanced University Types: Beyond the basic four groups, your professor expects you to know these advanced terms:

  • Real vs. Money (Pecuniary): Real externalities cause physical changes to health or nature (like breathing polluted air). Pecuniary externalities only change market prices (like a popular new factory making local rent prices go up). The big rule: only real externalities count as market failures — pecuniary ones are just normal market competition.
  • Local vs. Global: A noisy neighbor drilling the wall (local) vs. CO₂ gas destroying the Earth's climate (global). Local problems can be fixed by talking to neighbors; global problems need international laws.
  • Common Resource Problem: Being greedy with a shared, limited object (like catching too many fish until the ocean is dead).
  • Positional Externality: When buying things is just about social status — your neighbor buying a shiny Ferrari mathematically makes your Honda suddenly look pathetic.
  • Network Externalities: A service (like Instagram) suddenly gets much more fun and useful only because millions of your friends joined it.

The Math Definition

Famous economists Buchanan and Stubblebine (1962) used happiness formulas (utility functions) to explain this. If person A's happiness ($uA$) is directly changed by person B's action ($Y1$):

$$uA = uA(X_1, X_2, ..., X_m, Y_1)$$

An externality is mathematically proven if B's action genuinely shifts A's happiness up or down:

$$\frac{\partial uA}{\partial Y_1} \neq 0$$

• Positive: If $\partial uA / \partial Y_1 > 0$ (it brings joy/benefit).
• Negative: If $\partial uA / \partial Y_1 < 0$ (it brings pain/cost).

Selfish Value vs. Society Value

To fix the economy, we must strictly separate what the selfish individual pays/gets and what the entire country pays/gets.

Vocabulary Term What it Means
PMC Private Marginal Cost: The selfish factory cost.
SMC Social Marginal Cost: PMC + The pain inflicted on innocent people.
PMB Private Marginal Benefit: The selfish customer happiness.
SMB Social Marginal Benefit: PMB + The accidental blessing given to others.

The Math Rules:

  • 1. Perfect World (No side-effects): $PMC = SMC$ and $PMB = SMB$.
  • 2. Negative Externality (Pollution): $PMC < SMC$ (the company is cheating on its true costs).
  • 3. Positive Externality (Vaccines): $PMB < SMB$ (the buyer is giving free health to others).

The Four Ways Markets Fail: Knowing whether the market makes too much or too little helps politicians choose the right law.
• Negative Production $\rightarrow$ Making Too Much (factories don't pay for the smoke, so they naturally over-produce trash).
• Positive Production $\rightarrow$ Making Too Little (farmers don't get paid for their bees helping others, so they buy too few bees).
• Negative Consumption $\rightarrow$ Using Too Much (smokers ignore how their smoke hurts babies, so they smoke too much).
• Positive Consumption $\rightarrow$ Using Too Little (people ignore how their vaccine protects grandpas, so they buy too few vaccines).
In every single case, the selfish market result ($Q_1$) misses the perfect society goal ($Q^*$), creating a tragic triangle of wasted value called Deadweight Loss (DWL).

Reading the Graphs

1. Negative Production Damage (Example: Dirty Factories)

How to Read This:

  • Demand Line: $D = PMB = SMB$ (The desire line is normal).
  • Selfish Supply Line: $S = PMC$ (Only covers the factory's cheap bills).
  • Society Cost Line: $SMC = PMC + MD$ (Drawn higher up because it adds the painful Marginal Damage).

➡️ The Tragedy: The free market stops at $Q_1$ (where it thinks $PMC = PMB$), but the true healthy goal is at $Q^*$. The nasty factory creates too much product, creating a red triangle of pain (DWL) between the $SMC$ and the Demand line.

2. Negative Consumption Damage (Example: Cigarettes)

How to Read This:

  • Selfish Demand Line: $D = PMB$ (The smoker's personal joy).
  • Society Demand Line: $SMB = PMB - MD$ (Drawn much lower because the smoke hurts the waitress’s lungs).
  • Supply Line: $S = PMC = SMC$ (The factory is clean, so supply is normal).

➡️ The Tragedy: Selfish smokers totally ignore the damage ($MD$), buying a huge terrible quantity at $Q_1$ instead of the safer $Q_2$. This over-smoking creates a DWL triangle of suffering between the $SMB$ and the Supply line.

3. Positive Consumption Blessing (Example: Medical Vaccines / Education)

How to Read This:

  • Selfish Demand: $D = PMB$ (The person only cares about their own body).
  • Normal Supply : $S = PMC$ (Normal factory price).
  • Society Cost line (Professor's trick method): $SMC = PMC - MB$ (The beautiful external blessing is mathematically treated as a discount on the cost).

➡️ The Tragedy: Because selfish people don't care about giving free blessings to strangers ($MB$), they only buy a tiny amount at $Q_1$, while society begs for a massive amount at $Q_2$. The free market results in sad under-vaccination. Fixing this missing blessing fixes the DWL triangle.

Real World Proof: COVID-19

The COVID-19 disaster perfectly taught the world why externalities matter. Trying to stop a virus is packed with positive consumption blessings.

The Magic Actions

Wearing masks, washing hands, staying home, and getting vaccines.

The Human Problem

Benefit to Society is way bigger than Personal Benefit. Young healthy people refused to wear masks because it was annoying to them (personal cost), even though their mask could literally save ten grandmothers (massive society benefit).

Because humans are selfish, leaving it to the "free market" resulted in horrible under-protection. Politicians had to use heavy weapons:

  • Police Rules: Strict laws forcing masks and brutal lockdowns.
  • Free Money (Subsidies): Making vaccines 100% free for everyone.

Fixing the Bug (Internalizing)

💡 Famous Mistake: Most students wrongly think negative pollution should be banned and reduced to exactly ZERO.

In reality, economics proves the perfect pollution amount is never zero. Driving to the hospital creates car pollution, but saving a dying patient is so incredibly valuable that we are extremely happy to accept the little cloud of dirty air. We only want to balance the bad costs against the good rewards to find the "perfectly acceptable" limit.

Internalizing simply means passing a harsh law or tax that forces the greedy person to personally pay for the damage they cause, so they finally make a fair decision.

The Magical Double Dividend (Two Wins with One Tax)

Usually, when governments invent a new tax, it damages the economy (deadweight loss). But taxing nasty externalities is actually a magic trick.

How the Famous "Pigouvian Tax" Works:

  • Demand : $D = PMB = SMB$
  • Selfish Supply : $S = PMC$
  • True Social Cost : $SMC$ (floating high above $PMC$)

The Tax Fix : The government drops a special Pigouvian Tax ($T$) that exactly matches the damage gap between the $SMC$ and the $PMC$. This painful tax physically drags the supply curve upward to meet the $SMC$. It perfectly forces the factory to slow down from the disastrous $Q_1$ level straight down to the golden healthy $Q^*$ level.

This legendary tax creates two massive wins at the exact same time:

  • Win 1 (Fixing the damage): The tax kills the pollution and deletes the red DWL triangle of suffering. The air is clean again.
  • Win 2 (Free Government Money): The factory pays billions in tax money, which the government can heavily drop into building beautiful schools and hospitals.
💰 Unlike literally every other tax in history that slows down an economy, this special tax heals the country while printing free money for public projects.

Externalities Master Rule:

  • Ugly negative side-effects trick the market into making too much / buying too much trash.
  • Beautiful positive side-effects trick the market into making too little / buying too little goodness.
  • Internalizing is forcing a selfish person to feel the true pain of what they are doing to strangers.
  • Double Dividend: The ultimate Pigouvian Tax magic trick — healing the environment while harvesting millions in cash to build schools.

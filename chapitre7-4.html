Chapter 7: Health Economics — Theory, Systems, and the Cost of Aging

The World is Getting Too Old

The entire world is fighting a giant demographic crisis. Because people are living longer and having fewer babies, there are suddenly too many old people. Hospital systems were built for young people, and now they are crashing under the weight of the elderly.

61.2M

Americans aged 65+ right now

18.0%

Share of total US population

1.7B

Global old people (65+) by 2053

  • By 2030: 1 out of every 5 Americans (20.7%) will be over 65 years old.
  • By 2060: the older population will double; the super-old (85+) will triple.
  • The real tragedy is in poor countries. Places like Europe got rich before they got old. Poor countries today are getting old before they have the money to build nursing homes.

Old People Cost Too Much

Healthcare money is not shared equally. A small group of very old people secretly burns almost all of the country's medical budget, mostly because their bodies have so many permanent problems.

16% → 37%

65+ are 16% of people, but burn 37% of the money

95%

Over-60s with a permanent sickness

  • 79% of people over 60 have many permanent sicknesses at the exact same time (like diabetes + bad heart + memory loss).
  • If you have 4 permanent sicknesses, you cost the government $21,342 every year. If you are healthy, you only cost $2,025. That is a massive 10x difference.
  • A person aged 85 costs almost double the price of a person aged 65.
  • These numbers are driven by management (buying daily pills and nurses over 10 years), not by emergency surgery.

Is Age Actually The Problem? (The Debate)

When healthcare gets too expensive, everyone blames old people. But economists argue heavily about this. Are grandmas actually the problem?

🐟 The "Red Herring" (Fake Argument)

Zweifel (1999) argued that being old does not cost money; DYING costs money. If you die at age 40, your last month in the hospital costs $50,000. If you die at age 90, your last month also costs $50,000. So age is a "red herring" (a fake clue). The true cost is simply how close you are to the graveyard.

📈 The "Sick Life" Argument

Other experts say age absolutely matters because nursing homes are expensive. We are keeping 85-year-olds alive with medicine, but they are disabled and need a paid nurse to feed them every single day for 10 years. That costs vastly more than a quick death.

📊 The 2025 Truth: We now know that old people only cause 20% of the price increases. The other 80% is because hospitals keep inventing incredibly expensive new technologies (like robot surgery and luxury drugs).

The Final Answer (Breyer, 2019)

Friedrich Breyer (2019) solved the great debate. He proved that both sides are right, depending on the bill.

  • Hospital Prices (Surgery): The "Red Herring" theory is right. Hospital costs explode simply because you are near death, no matter if you are 40 or 90.
  • Nursing Home Prices: The "Demographic" theory is right. Normal 40-year-olds never go to nursing homes. But an 85-year-old will live there for years. Age absolutely drives this cost.
🎯 The Government Warning: Governments must stop acting like hospitals and nursing homes are the same thing. Nursing homes are facing a true demographic explosion and will soon bankrupt the system if we don't plan for it immediately.

Breyer, F. (2019). Health Care and Aging. Oxford Research Encyclopedia.

The USA Medicare Time Bomb

Medicare is the public hospital card for all Americans over 65. It is the perfect example of a fiscal time bomb waiting to explode:

2033

The year the money completely runs out

>5%

Medicare's massive bite out of the total US economy

  • The national bank account that pays for Medicare will be completely empty by 2033. When this happens, hospitals will suddenly receive 11% less money.
  • The largest generation in history (the "Baby Boomers") are all turning 65 at the exact same time, flooding the system.
  • The Core Problem: To keep paying for these old people, America will have to heavily cut spending on the military, roads, and schools.

Weapons Governments Can Use

How can governments stop the system from collapsing? They have a toolbox of legal weapons:

The Weapon Real World Example Why It Works
Legal Force (Mandates) Swiss law Stops the Death Spiral — forces young, healthy people to pay into the pool.
Giving Cash (Subsidies) ObamaCare discounts Creates fairness — makes sure the poor don't die just because they can't afford a ticket.
Price Control Government drug price list Stops greeds — blocks giant pharmaceutical companies from charging $1,000 for a $5 pill.
Risk Balancing Medicare Advantage scores Stops cheating — if an insurance company purposely takes only healthy people, the government fines them.
One Buyer (Single Payer) Canada Mega discounts — if there is only one buyer (the government), they demand massive discounts from doctors.
Owning the Hospital UK NHS Total cost control — the government simply tells doctors exactly what their salary is, and they cannot argue.

The World Bank Rule for Poorer Nations

Poorer countries do not have enough money to build a giant UK-style system. So the World Bank (1999) gave them a simple playbook to follow:

  • The Golden Rule: The government should pay the bills, but not own the hospital. It is too hard for poor governments to run a hospital properly.
  • The government should only focus on basic survival: give everyone vaccines and stop deadly viruses from spreading.
  • Let private doctors build the hospitals, but the government must create strict safety rules so people aren't scammed.
  • You cannot copy-paste the German system into Uganda. You must build a system that matches the country's actual wealth.
